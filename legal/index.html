How it plays

Watch. Then tap it back.

The same loop the game runs: one bar is played to you, the next one is yours. No bar waits in between.

Listen

Four counts, then the hammer plays.

  1. Top row is the hammer’s. Each beat it plays lights a bead.
  2. Bottom row is yours. When the token lands there, tap the same beats.

Watch first

Every task opens with the act playing its own bar. Nothing is written down.

One tap

Tap anywhere. There is no second button and nothing to aim at.

Judged on time

Perfect inside ±55 ms, Good inside ±130 ms, measured against what you hear.

It keeps going

Tempo, length and pattern all ride one curve that never quite flattens.

Twenty-eight acts

Everyday scenes. Hidden charts.

One act per level, in order, then round again — the same scene at a tempo that has moved on. Every clip is the game itself, recorded on the act’s first level: the demonstration, a clean answer, and the table sliding on to the next.

For rhythm game lovers

Windows you can feel.

The numbers under the game, in the open — because the people who care about them are the people this is for.

The judge

early late
  • Perfect ±55 ms
  • Good ±130 ms
  • Miss outside, or one tap too many

Judged against the sample you are hearing, not the one the device is scheduling — and there is a calibration screen for the gap between them.

The climb

120→150

Every level opens at the music's tempo and ramps task by task. Length grows from three tasks to eight.
